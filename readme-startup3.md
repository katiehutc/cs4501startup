## Pally for investment banking recruiting
Original startup: Pally is an AI personal assistant that connects to your inboxes, calendar, files, and tasks to keep track of your connections and help you prepare for meetings. Link: https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/pally

Proposed startup: An AI-powered networking tracker for college students recruiting for investment banking. It turns pasted emails, LinkedIn profile text, and post-call notes into a structured pipeline of contacts, coffee chats, and follow-ups. It also schedules chats around the user's calendar, sends reminders, and writes prep briefs and thank-you drafts.

Criteria:
1. Solves one problem extremely well:
Keeps an IB candidate's networking pipeline organized and moving so no follow-up is missed and no coffee chat goes unprepared. Candidates often network with dozens of bankers across many firms and groups, usually tracked in a spreadsheet that falls out of date. The product focuses only on this loop: outreach, scheduling, prep, notes, thank-you, follow-up. It is not a job board or resume builder.
2. Access to target customers:
Students recruiting for IB summer analyst roles are directly accessible through UVA classmates, McIntire, and finance and investment clubs, many of whom network every recruiting cycle. They can test the tool on their real pipelines during the current recruiting season.
3. Familiarity with infrastructure:
The MVP can use a React/Next.js frontend, a Python (FastAPI) backend, PostgreSQL through Supabase, Google OAuth and the Google Calendar API, scheduled jobs for reminders, an email service for notifications, and an LLM API with structured JSON output. These are common, well-documented tools.
4. Reimagines the problem space:
Today's spreadsheets are passive: the student does all the data entry, remembers every deadline, and writes every email from scratch. This tool is active. A student pastes messy notes such as "talked w/ Jake, 2nd yr analyst GS TMT SF, said follow up in a month and reach out to his VP Priya." The system creates the contact record, logs the chat, adds Priya as a referral lead, schedules the follow-up, and drafts the thank-you note. A per-firm dashboard then shows where the student stands with each bank.
5. LLM is core to the solution:
The LLM converts unstructured notes, email threads, and profile text into structured contact, interaction, and task records. It normalizes shorthand ("GS TMT SF" becomes Goldman Sachs, Technology/Media/Telecom, San Francisco) and infers next steps and timing from what was said. It also writes personalized prep briefs and email drafts. Without the LLM, the user would have to fill in every field by hand, which is the main reason spreadsheet trackers get abandoned.
6. Validation guardrail:
Every LLM output must pass a validator before the user sees it. Records must match a JSON schema, and firm and group names must resolve to a maintained database of banks and groups; unknown firms are flagged, never invented. Titles must be valid IB titles, and duplicate contacts are blocked. Dates must be in the future, use the correct time zones, avoid conflicts on the user's Google Calendar, and follow cadence rules (e.g., at least 5 business days between follow-ups to the same person). Drafts and prep briefs go through a grounding check: every claim about a banker must appear in the user's notes or pasted profile text. The recipient's name and firm must match the contact record, which prevents mailing the wrong bank's name, and no template placeholders may remain. Recruiting timeline alerts may only use dates from a maintained timeline table. Emails are never sent automatically; the user reviews and sends them.
7. Self-correction loop:
Validation errors are automatically sent back to the LLM along with the original input and the failed fields. For example: "firm 'GS' did not resolve; did you mean Goldman Sachs?", "the draft mentions a deal not found in the notes", or "follow-up date is in the past." The model regenerates within a limit of 3 retries. If the output still fails, the system asks the user a targeted question (e.g., "Is 'GS' Goldman Sachs or Guggenheim Securities?") instead of showing an unverified result. Failures are logged by type to improve prompts over time.
8. Deployability:
The full product can be deployed with a hosted frontend (Vercel), a backend (Render), Supabase for the database and auth, and an LLM API, all on free or student tiers. Google Calendar access can run in Google's OAuth testing mode for a beta of up to 100 users. Users paste LinkedIn text themselves, so no scraping is needed. The bank/group database and recruiting timeline are small tables the team seeds and maintains.
